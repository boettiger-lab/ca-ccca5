California Plant Heat Vulnerability

Where, within a plant species' current range, do projected air temperatures reach the point at which its photosynthesis is measurably impaired?

The question

Plant heat tolerance is measured in the lab as two leaf temperatures. Tcrit is where photosynthetic capacity is first impaired; T50 is where performance falls by half. They are properties of the leaf, measured on the leaf.

Climate projections, however, give air temperature. A sunlit leaf is usually warmer than the air around it, by an amount that depends on leaf size, wind, and how much water the plant is transpiring. So the two cannot be compared directly. This tool asks you for that difference — the leaf-to-air offset — and does the comparison in air temperature:

air-temperature threshold = (Tcrit or T50) − leaf-to-air offset

The offset is your assumption, not a measurement, and it matters enormously. Across the 109 species in this study the median Tcrit is about 47 °C. Assume no offset and almost nowhere in California qualifies; assume 10 °C and much of the Central Valley and the deserts do. Every answer states the offset it used, so two results can be compared.

Exceedance is exposure, not death. A cell above the threshold is a place where the species would experience temperatures past a measured physiological limit. It is not a prediction of mortality, dieback, or range loss, and nothing here should be read that way.

What the range layers show

A species' range here is every cell the Calflora climate model finds suitable — not only the places the plant has been seen. The number in a cell is how many times the species has actually been recorded there: a cell that is climatically suitable but has no record carries a zero, and is still inside the range. Zeros are about 98% of it, which is why a range reads as a broad pale extent with scattered darker cells where the herbarium records are.

That scatter is survey effort — where botanists have looked — not abundance. The count stops at 9, meaning nine or more. Ranges are not clipped to the state line: several species have genuine records in Baja California, including a white fir population in the Sierra San Pedro Mártir.

What the interim climate data can and cannot tell you

The tool is designed around mean annual days above the threshold — a frequency. That needs daily projections, and the LOCA2 build is not finished.

In the meantime the map uses CHELSA's maximum air temperature of the warmest month, which happens to be published on exactly the same hexagonal cells as the species ranges, so the two join directly. It answers whether a place gets that hot and by how much — but never how often, or for how long. Treat a result as a flag for where to look, not as a dose.

Thermal tolerance values are not loaded yet. The project team's measurements are cleared for publication but have not been added, so you will need to give a Tcrit or T50 value yourself. That is how the tool was always meant to work — different populations, experiments and sources give different numbers — but until the table lands there is no default to fall back on.

Running the computation

Ask in plain language. A question that works today:

Where does Artemisia californica's range get hotter than its Tcrit of 44.6 °C, assuming leaves run 10 °C above air? Gives the share of the range above threshold, the largest exceedance, and a map.
Same species and offset, but under SSP3-7.0 at end of century — how much more of the range is affected? On the observed baseline 2.4% of the range is above threshold; by 2071–2100 under SSP3-7.0 it is 28.1%.
Sweep the leaf-to-air offset from 0 to 15 °C and chart how the exposed share changes. Shows how much the answer depends on the assumption.

Underneath, that is a join between the two datasets on a shared hexagonal grid:

WITH r AS (
  SELECT h8 FROM read_parquet(
    's3://public-ca-ccca5/calflora-ranges/hex/h0=*/data_0.parquet',
    hive_partitioning = true)
  WHERE species_code = 'ARTCAL'
)
SELECT count(*)                                        AS range_cells,
       sum(CASE WHEN c.bio5 > 34.57 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS cells_over,
       round(max(c.bio5) - 34.57, 1)                   AS max_margin_c
FROM r JOIN read_parquet(
  's3://public-bioclimate/chelsa-2-1/baseline-1981-2010/hex/h0=*/data_0.parquet') c
  USING (h8)

The denominator is the species' whole range, zeros included, and it is stated in every answer alongside the offset — a percentage means nothing without knowing what it is a percentage of.
